📊 What is Schema Markup?

Schema markup (structured data) is code you add to your HTML that helps search engines understand your content better. It enables rich results in Google — star ratings, FAQ dropdowns, event dates, price information, and more directly in search results — increasing visibility and CTR dramatically.

JSON-LD (the format this tool generates) is the recommended format by Google. You simply paste the generated code inside your page's <head> section. No HTML modification needed — it's completely separate from your visible content.

Schema Types Explained

  1. FAQ — Accordion-style questions appear directly in Google
  2. Article — Author, date and publisher in search results
  3. Product — Price, ratings and availability in results
  4. LocalBusiness — Address, hours, phone in knowledge panel
  5. BreadcrumbList — Navigation trail shown below title
  6. Event — Date, venue and ticket info in results

Why JSON-LD Instead of Microdata or RDFa | Microdata या RDFa के बजाय JSON-LD क्यों

Structured data can technically be written in three formats — JSON-LD, Microdata, or RDFa — but Google explicitly recommends JSON-LD because it's a single self-contained block that doesn't need to be interleaved inside your visible HTML. This means you can add, update or remove schema without touching your page's actual content or layout, which is exactly how this tool's output is designed to be used — just pasted into your page's <head>. | Structured data को technically तीन formats में लिखा जा सकता है — JSON-LD, Microdata, या RDFa — लेकिन Google specifically JSON-LD की recommend करता है क्योंकि यह एक self-contained block है जिसे आपके visible HTML के भीतर interleave करने की ज़रूरत नहीं होती। इसका मतलब है कि आप अपने page के actual content या layout को छुए बिना schema add, update या remove कर सकते हैं — जो exactly इस टूल के output के उपयोग का तरीका है — बस इसे अपने page के <head> में paste करें।

Google decides whether and how to show rich results based on its own quality signals, and these eligibility rules change over time (for example, Google restricted FAQ rich results to a narrower set of sites in 2023).
