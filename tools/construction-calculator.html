ℹ️ About This Tool

Accurately estimating construction materials before starting work saves significant money and avoids project delays from running short. Buying too little means a second trip and possible batch-mismatch issues for tiles or paint; buying too much ties up capital in unused stock. This calculator covers the four most commonly needed estimates in residential construction and renovation.

The Cement calculator uses M20 mix proportions (1:1.5:3), which is the standard for RCC slabs in residential construction. It accounts for the dry volume factor (1.54×) since concrete shrinks during setting. The Brick calculator uses standard Indian modular brick dimensions (230×115×75 mm) with a 10mm mortar joint. Mortar volume is calculated as 35% of brick volume.

The Paint calculator uses an industry-standard coverage of 14 m² per litre per coat (150 sq ft/litre), typical for interior emulsion paints — adjust for higher-quality paints with better coverage or textured surfaces with lower coverage. The Tile calculator accounts for the common 10% wastage due to cuts at room edges, which is especially significant for large format tiles in complex room shapes.

How to use:

  1. Select the material type (Cement, Bricks, Paint, or Tiles).
  2. Enter the dimensions of the area or project.
  3. Click Calculate to see material quantities needed.

One important note for all these calculations: they produce theoretical material requirements based on standard dimensions and formulae. Real construction sites should add contingency buffers beyond the built-in wastage — materials can be damaged in transit, sizes may not be exactly as specified, or on-site cuts and adjustments use more than calculated. Discussing the estimates with your contractor or mason before purchasing is always wise; experienced builders often have rules of thumb for local material variations that supplement these standard calculations. Also note that material quality varies significantly — a higher-grade cement or paint with better coverage may justify a higher price per unit even if the quantity needed is similar, because durability and finish quality affect long-term maintenance costs. For larger projects, getting quotes from multiple material suppliers and comparing per-unit costs alongside these quantity estimates allows you to benchmark whether a contractor's materials bill is in the right range before accepting any quotation. These estimates also serve as a useful cross-check when reviewing contractor bills — if a contractor's quoted material quantities significantly exceed these calculated figures for the same area, it warrants clarification before approving payment. Multiplying these estimated quantities by current market rates for materials in your area gives a reliable ballpark for the total materials cost component of any construction or renovation project budget.

Frequently Asked Questions | अक्सर पूछे जाने वाले प्रश्न

Q. Why does the Cement tab multiply the volume by 1.54? | Cement tab volume को 1.54 से क्यों multiply करता है?
Dry cement, sand and aggregate occupy about 54% more volume before mixing than the finished wet concrete does, so this "dry volume factor" corrects for that shrinkage during setting. | Dry cement, sand और aggregate mixing से पहले finished wet concrete से लगभग 54% ज़्यादा volume लेते हैं, इसलिए यह "dry volume factor" setting के दौरान होने वाली shrinkage को correct करता है।

Q. What does the 1:1.5:3 ratio in the Cement tab mean? | Cement tab में 1:1.5:3 ratio का क्या मतलब है?
It is the M20 concrete mix ratio of cement to sand to aggregate by volume, a standard mix used for residential RCC slabs, beams and columns. | यह M20 concrete mix का cement, sand और aggregate का volume के हिसाब से ratio है, जो residential RCC slabs, beams और columns के लिए standard mix है।

Q. Why 50kg bags for cement? | Cement के लिए 50kg bags क्यों?
50kg is the standard retail cement bag size sold across India, so the calculator converts the required cement volume directly into that familiar unit. | 50kg भारत में बिकने वाले standard retail cement bag का size है, इसलिए calculator ज़रूरी cement volume को सीधे इस जाने-पहचाने unit में convert करता है।

Q. What brick size does the Brick tab assume? | Brick tab कौन-सा brick size मानता है?
It assumes the standard Indian modular brick of 230×115×75 mm with a 10mm mortar joint on each side, which is the most commonly available size. | यह standard Indian modular brick 230×115×75 mm को 10mm mortar joint के साथ मानता है, जो सबसे commonly available size है।

Q. Which wall thickness option should I choose? | मुझे कौन-सा wall thickness option चुनना चाहिए?
Half-brick (4.5") suits non-load-bearing partition walls, one-brick (9") is the standard for most load-bearing exterior walls, and one-and-a-half brick (13.5") is used for heavier load-bearing structures. | Half-brick (4.5") non-load-bearing partition walls के लिए उपयुक्त है, one-brick (9") ज़्यादातर load-bearing exterior walls के लिए standard है, और one-and-a-half brick (13.5") heavier load-bearing structures के लिए इस्तेमाल होता है।

Q. Why does the Brick tab add 10% wastage? | Brick tab 10% wastage क्यों जोड़ता है?
Breakage during transport, handling and on-site cutting around corners and openings typically accounts for about 10% extra bricks, so budgeting for it avoids a mid-project shortage. | Transport, handling और corners/openings के पास on-site cutting के दौरान टूटने से typically लगभग 10% extra bricks लगते हैं, इसलिए इसके लिए पहले से budget रखना project के बीच में कमी से बचाता है।

Q. What coverage does the Paint tab assume? | Paint tab कौन-सी coverage मानता है?
It uses 14 m² (about 150 sq ft) per litre per coat, the typical coverage for standard interior emulsion paint on a reasonably smooth wall. | यह 14 m² (लगभग 150 sq ft) प्रति litre प्रति coat इस्तेमाल करता है, जो reasonably smooth दीवार पर standard interior emulsion paint की typical coverage है।

Q. Why do I need to enter a doors+windows area for paint? | Paint के लिए मुझे doors+windows area क्यों डालना है?
Doors and windows are not painted the same way as walls, so subtracting their area from the total wall area gives the actual paintable surface. | Doors और windows दीवारों की तरह paint नहीं किए जाते, इसलिए उनका area कुल wall area से घटाने पर actual paintable surface मिलता है।

Q. Why does the Tile tab round up the tile count? | Tile tab tile count को ऊपर की ओर क्यों round करता है?
Tiles must be bought in whole units and the 10% wastage buffer accounts for cuts needed at room edges and around fixtures, so rounding up avoids running short mid-job. | Tiles पूरी units में ही खरीदी जाती हैं और 10% wastage buffer room edges और fixtures के पास ज़रूरी cuts को cover करता है, इसलिए ऊपर round करना काम के बीच में कमी से बचाता है।
